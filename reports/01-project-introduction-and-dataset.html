1. Project introduction

This project builds a production-style FMCG retail Data & AI platform using real household shopping data. The business problem is not “train a model for its own sake.” It is: retailers lack a trusted, end-to-end path from raw transactions to demand insight, promotion evaluation, and management questions answered from governed data.

You will ingest and clean retail data in Databricks (Delta Lake), model analytics with dbt, visualise KPIs in Power BI, forecast demand with MLflow-tracked models, and later add a grounded AI assistant that only queries trusted Gold tables.

Medallion Bronze → Silver → Gold on Databricks
Stack PySpark, dbt, Power BI, MLflow, GenAI
Domain Sales, promos, customers, demand
Outcome Trusted marts + forecasts + AI Q&A

Main analytical questions

  • Which categories, stores, and products drive sales?
  • Which promotions and coupons actually lift demand?
  • Which products need replenishment priority, and where is demand volatile?
  • Can we forecast demand and flag abnormal spikes or drops?
  • Can managers ask these questions in natural language against trusted data?

2. What to do (recommended order)

Do not start with the AI chatbot. The AI layer sits on top of clean, tested Gold data. Follow this sequence so each step has something solid to stand on.

  1. Prep (you are here / just finished raw data). Repo structure, local Python env, Databricks workspace, catalog fmcg with schemas bronze, silver, gold, ml, ai.
  2. Explore. Profile every CSV: row counts, nulls, keys, duplicates. Write notebook 01_data_exploration.
  3. Bronze. Load raw files into Delta with ingestion metadata only (no heavy business logic). Milestone: all sources in fmcg.bronze.*.
  4. Silver. Clean types, remove invalid rows, standardise IDs, derive sales/discount flags.
  5. dbt Gold. Staging → intermediate → dimensions, facts, and marts. Add tests.
  6. Power BI. Connect only to Gold marts (never Bronze).
  7. ML. Demand features, baseline + stronger models, MLflow tracking, batch forecasts.
  8. AI Copilot. SQL assistant over allow-listed Gold tables, evaluation, responsible AI.
MVP if time is limited: Bronze → Silver → dbt Gold → Power BI → demand forecast + MLflow → basic AI SQL assistant. Promotion-response models, Feature Store, and Model Serving can wait.

3. Data source

Source name: dunnhumby — The Complete Journey.

Dunnhumby is a customer-science company known for retail loyalty analytics. The Complete Journey is a widely used public learning dataset of anonymised household grocery purchasing behaviour over roughly two years of relative time (days and weeks are numbered from the start of the study, not a public calendar year).

It is designed for retail analytics education: basket-level transactions, product hierarchy, in-store / mailer promotion signals, coupon campaigns, and household demographic segments.

What the source is good for

  • Sales and category performance analysis
  • Promotion and coupon effectiveness studies
  • Customer segmentation (RFM and demographic bands)
  • Demand / velocity modelling at store × product × week grain

What to remember about the source

  • Relative time: columns like DAY and WEEK_NO are study-relative. For analytics you often map them to a synthetic calendar so dashboards have dates.
  • Anonymisation: household keys are IDs, not real people. Demographic fields in this export may appear as classification_* bands instead of classic labels like AGE_DESC.
  • Scale: transactions are large (~2.6M rows). Causal/promotion data is much larger (~37M rows). Plan Spark/Databricks compute accordingly; do not try to open causal_data.csv fully in Excel.
Local + Databricks layout: Local CSVs live under fmcg-retail-ai-platform/data/raw/ (gitignored). On Databricks they are available at /Volumes/fmcg/bronze/raw (confirmed by exploration). The zip archive sits one level above the project folder.

4.4 Coupons — coupon.csv & coupon_redempt.csv

  • coupon.csv: which coupon UPC applies to which product in which campaign (COUPON_UPC, PRODUCT_ID, CAMPAIGN).
  • coupon_redempt.csv: actual redemptions by household and day (household_key, DAY, COUPON_UPC, CAMPAIGN).

4.5 Campaigns — campaign_desc.csv & campaign_table.csv

  • campaign_desc.csv: campaign metadata — DESCRIPTION (e.g. TypeA/B/C), CAMPAIGN, START_DAY, END_DAY.
  • campaign_table.csv: targeting — which household_key received which CAMPAIGN / type.

4.6 hh_demographic.csv — household segments

About 800 households with demographic attributes. In this export, several fields are anonymised as classification_1 … classification_5, plus HOMEOWNER_DESC, KID_CATEGORY_DESC, and household_key.

Treat these as segment labels for analytics and AI — avoid exposing or stressing individual-level stories.

5. How the files relate

Think of a simple join map:

  • transaction_data.PRODUCT_ID → product.PRODUCT_ID
  • transaction_data.household_key → hh_demographic.household_key (not all households may appear in both)
  • transaction_data × causal_data on product + store + week for promo context
  • campaign_* + coupon* for campaign response and redemption analysis

In the medallion design: these CSVs land in Bronze as-is (plus ingestion metadata), become cleaned entities in Silver, and feed dimensional models / marts in Gold via dbt.

6. Practical notes before you start coding

  1. Explore with samples first (limit / small fractions), especially for causal_data.csv.
  2. Decide key quality rules early: non-null product/store/basket IDs, non-negative quantity and sales.
  3. Document your day→date mapping when you invent a synthetic calendar.
  4. Keep Power BI and the AI assistant pointed at Gold only.
